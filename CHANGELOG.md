# Spyro Editor 1.5

## Changes since Spyro Editor 1.4

New

- Game Settings › Spyro's colours: give Spyro any colours you like, part by part: body, belly, horns, the spiky fins behind his head, wing frame, wing skin, tail tip, eyes and pupils. A picture of Spyro on the card shows the look as you choose it, and each part keeps his shading. Pick a colour from the quick colours, with the hue, richness and brightness sliders or as #RRGGBB, start from a look (Ember, Ice, Emerald, Gold, Shadow; Classic puts him back), or click Surprise me for one made up on the spot. The colours are in every level, on the title screen, the loading screens and in the cutscenes of the games you create.
- Create BIN makes a game from Game Settings alone (Spyro's colours, Hover), with no level edits needed.
- Game Settings › Hover: Spyro 3's hover in the games you create. Pressing ▲ while gliding makes Spyro flutter: he beats his wings quickly (the flap from the flight levels, at three times its speed), rises about as high as a short jump and drifts on a little, then drops; as in Spyro 2 and 3 he can't glide again until he lands. Off unless you tick it. Create BIN adds the flap to every level that has room for it on a PlayStation (4.5 KB); Tree Tops, Haunted Towers and Twilight Harbor have none, and there Spyro hovers without flapping.

- Random Level… is rebuilt on the game's own level logic. It used to stack blocks on a grid, which looked nothing like a Spyro level. Now a level is laid out as the game's levels are (rooms along a route that loops back over the start, off a big clearing, or along a winding trail; sloping paths; steps, lookouts to glide from, pools, hidden nooks, charge ramps, tunnels), built as they are built (one sheet of four-cornered faces about 128 units across whose lines follow the land, inside steep banks, rock walls, peaks, water or sky) and painted as they are painted: you pick a level to make it "in the style of" (eighteen of the game's levels, from Artisans to Twilight Harbor), and the new level uses the textures that level itself uses for its ground, banks, rock and paths, in the colours that level paints them with, under its sky, to its music. With your project's levels loaded, that level's own towers, trees and rocks are stood in the new level. Every floor is still proved reachable before the level is shown.
- Random levels have a difficulty: Easy, Medium, Hard or Kaizo. Easy levels have wide paths, bridges and short hops; hard ones narrow paths, stepping stones, more ledges and lookouts, glides with little to spare and pools that cost a life at once; Kaizo leaves next to nothing to spare. Every jump and glide stays inside what Spyro can do, and every floor is still proved reachable.
- File ▾ › Random Homeworld… makes a homeworld: a plaza where Spyro arrives, rooms off it all round, and a doorway (frame and all) to every level the homeworld leads to, each in the middle of a room with open floor in front and behind for Spyro to come back out onto. In Artisans' place it leads to Artisans' five levels; in your own world's homeworld, to each of your levels and back out to the game (the game's other homeworlds are not offered yet). The doorways' look follows the level and differs every time: a shape of the look's kind (stone arches for the Artisans, square, slanted and gabled gates for the Peace Keepers, pointed and tall arches for the Magic Crafters, six-sided gates for the Beast Makers, rings for the Dream Weavers), each doorway its own size, framed in the level's own stone in the level's colours, with the destination's sky filling each opening exactly. Terrain › Doorways changes any of them afterwards.
- Objects › Random objects fills a level you built the way the game's own levels are filled, by the game's own measure (taken from its thirty levels on foot): pick a level type (whose animals and chests) and an enemy type (whose enemies, all kinds or one), a difficulty, how many gems the level should hold and how many dragonfly eggs, and press Randomize. For the gems you ask for there are about as many chests, enemies and loose gems as a level of the game with that treasure has (for every hundred gems about ten chests, seven enemies and sixteen loose gems); about a third of the treasure lies loose, nearly half is in chests and the rest comes from enemies; chests are mostly baskets and strong chests with some spring, three-flame and firework chests, an extra life, and a locked chest whose key lies somewhere else, each giving what its kind gives in the game. Loose gems are mostly reds and greens in ones, twos and threes a hundred units apart, a group all one colour, most of them by the walls, the best at the hardest places, with one of every colour, and the level's treasure comes to exactly the number you typed. Enemies wait in small groups in the rooms beyond the first and on the paths, animals graze in little groups, egg thieves get room to run, dragons are spread through the rooms and the way out stands at the far end (in a homeworld, the balloonist and his balloon). Nothing is put inside a tower, against a wall or in front of a doorway, and each kind is tried on in the level first, so what Randomize places always builds (a kind the level cannot hold is left out and named). Press Randomize again for another arrangement; Remove Them takes them away. The Random Level window's "Then fill it with objects for me" does this at once.
- Game Settings › Egg thieves' colour: the egg thieves' robes in any colour in place of their blue, in every level that has thieves and for thieves you add to levels you build. Pick a ready colour, type one as #RRGGBB or press Surprise me; the card draws a thief in it. (In High Caves the tornado wizards wear the same cloth, so they change with them.)
- An egg thief's run can be shaped: select a thief you added to a level you built and Edit Route… and the "Its run" tools appear (in the release build too, where they were missing): drag the run's points in the 3D view or type them, make a New Loop Here, make the run Bigger or Smaller, Turn it, send him the Other Way Round, or bring back the run As It Came. The same tools work for any added object that walks a route.
- Three more doorway shapes for homeworlds: Tall Arch, Gable Gate and Slanted Gate (nine in all).

Improved

- The hint over the 3D view has a dark backing, so it reads over a bright sky.
- The File menu is grouped: making and opening levels of your own, changing the open level, recovery, saving.
- The Doorways and World Entrance cards lay out their Move, Height and Turn buttons in rows, so no label is cut off.
- Game Settings is split into "This level" (name, music, portals) and "Whole game" (Spyro's colours, Hover, Fly Anywhere, Play as Crash, Memory).
- The welcome card points new users to Help and to Game Settings as a first stop.
- The Music card's Year of the Dragon disc button has its own row.
- Help covers Ground, Tile Type, Add beside, Doorways, World Entrance, Hover, Spyro's colours, Play as Crash, Memory, Random Level, Year of the Dragon levels and Fill In, with a section on making your own world.
