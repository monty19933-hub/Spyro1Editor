# Spyro Editor 2.4

## Changes since Spyro Editor 2.2

Version 2.3 was built but never released; everything it had is in 2.4.

New

- A **Colours** tab beside Game Settings holds everything about looks and moves: this level's colours, Spyro's colours, the egg thieves' colour, Hover, Fly Anywhere and Play as Crash. Memory, names, music and portals stay in Game Settings.
- **This level's colours** (it was Creatures' colours) now lists the open level's **chests**, **dragons** and **animals** as well as its enemies, each list with its own Randomize, and Randomize All for everything. A colour is for that level only, so chests can be blue in one level and red in the next. For dragons, one crystal colour goes on every dragon statue of the level (it used to be set statue by statue in the object's Edit window), and the fairy who waits at a freed dragon's pedestal can be given a colour too.
- **Randomize Every Level** on the same card gives the enemies, chests and animals of every level of the game other colours in one press, each level its own (263 kinds in 35 levels); **Every Level Its Own** puts them all back.
- Enemies and things with no colour of their own (grey metal, stone) can now be given one; they used to be missing from the list.
- **Egg thieves anywhere**: an egg thief added to a level that has none (Add Object › Enemies › Egg Thieves, then New Loop Here for his run) runs, can be caught and gives his egg, in the game's levels and homeworlds.
- **Every egg thief's run can be changed**, the game's own thieves too (select him, then Edit Run Path or drag the numbered points). A point goes onto the ground he runs on and is taken only where he can still run to it and on from it; otherwise it stays put and the editor says why. Water, lava and ooze count as no ground: he could run there, but Spyro sinks and can't catch him. The run card warns when a thief stands in water.
- **The egg count follows the thieves**: every egg thief you add is one more egg in the inventory, in the 100% count and in what Gnasty's Loot's door asks for, and a thief you take out is one fewer. Create BIN says how many eggs the game has when it is not the game's 12. A level without its own thief can still be finished 100%.
- **Spring chests that hold a teal or a rainbow gem**: Add Object › Interactive › Chests & Containers has **Spring chest (Teal reward)** and **Spring chest (Rainbow reward)**. Flame or charge one and the gem springs up out of the lid; grab it quickly or it vanishes and the chest closes again, as the game's spring chests do. It adds 50 or 100 to the treasure and to the level's total, in the game's own levels and in levels you build.
- **Random levels and homeworlds are far more varied**: each seed picks one of eleven layouts. Four of them are one piece of land of many parts at their own heights, the way most of the game's levels are built: **a wide land with raised and sunken parts** (like Artisans or Stone Hill), **a low floor with plateaus to climb** (like Dry Canyon), **terraces climbing a hillside** (like Magic Crafters) and **yards side by side at their own heights** (like Town Square or Lofty Castle). The others are a way from room to room with an end, a ring that comes back over its start, a clearing with rooms round it, one great room with a hill, mound or moat, a hill of terraces to climb, a winding gorge and a web of rooms with more than one way round. Rooms run from about 300 to 1,150 units across, with flights of steps, long climbing paths, stepped mounds, raised terraces, hollows, islets and moats. **Laid out as** in the Random Level window chooses the layout yourself. The same seed makes a different level than in 2.2.
- **Rooms are no longer round**: a room's edge is drawn as rock shelves with flat sides and corners, long terraces or squared yards (by the level's kind and look), straight across wherever a path or a flight of steps meets it.
- **Houses, castle walls, rivers and marshes** in random levels and homeworlds. Houses with pitched roofs, a door and windows stand round the rooms, a few together like a village, their backs to the room's edge; Spyro walks round them. Castle walls with a round tower at each end run along rooms' straight edges, with battlements, and a castle level's own walls get battlements along their tops. A river runs across a room from bank to bank (or out from under a higher part's wall), a little below the floor, with a wooden bridge wherever a way into the room crosses it and one to its far bank. A marsh at a room's side has a boardwalk (on Hard and Kaizo, stepping stones) out to an islet with treasure. Each is a new tick box in the Random Level window (**Rivers**, **Houses**, **Castle walls**; **Pools** is now **Pools and marshes**), left to chance by the level's kind unless you tick or untick it.
- **Water, ooze or lava**: the Random Level window chooses what a level's rivers, marshes, pools and moats are full of: water, the purple ooze or lava, or **Surprise me** (the look's own as a rule, now and then another). On Hard and Kaizo falling in costs a life at once.
- **Random homeworlds are one land with the doorways round it**, no longer a ring of round rooms on the ends of paths: a big part holds two or three doorways near its edge, backs to the bank, facing the land, and the open floor in front of a doorway may run on into the next part. Left to chance, a homeworld is laid out as one of the one-land layouts (or a web, ring, climb or journey); a clearing with rooms round it and one great room with rooms off it still come when chosen, with their rooms built right against the middle where they fit.
- **Random objects places things more like the game**: chests and gems up on raised places and perches, keys on perches, the extra life and locked chest tucked away, enemies spread through the rooms with treasure mostly out of their reach, gems in little knots as well as lines, and nothing on banks or in the sea.
- **Teal and rainbow gems in Random objects**: tick **Teal gems (50)** or **Rainbow gems (100)** and they go alone to the hardest places to reach, counted in the treasure you asked for.
- **Trees, torches, flowers** in Random objects (on unless unticked): the level type's own trees, torches, lamps, flowers and grass, placed where the game puts them.
- **Trodden paths** cross the rooms of random levels and homeworlds, from every way in (a path, a doorway, where Spyro starts) to a meeting place near the middle, in the look's path surface, carrying on the paths between rooms, as Artisans and Stone Hill have them. Gems lie in lines along them, and Random objects lines the paths and trodden paths with the level type's torches and lamp posts, a few hundred units apart on alternate sides.
- **Marshes far more often**: a level with pools and marshes (now most of them) has at least one, they fit smaller rooms, and islands in the sea or the sky can have them too.
- Random objects stands **trees in little groups along the banks**, leaving the rooms' middles open, and places more trees, lamps and flowers.
- **Music for random levels**: the Random Level window has a **Music** choice (the look's own songs by default), and **Game Settings › Music** now shows for a level you built, so its song can be changed afterwards.
- **Memory is Emulator (8 MB RAM) unless you choose otherwise**, so levels hold more and fewer changes run out of room. A game made this way still plays on a normal 2 MB set-up wherever its levels fit 2 MB, and Create BIN says when one does not. Choose **PlayStation (2 MB)** in Game Settings › Memory for a real console or a disc. A project that never chose now gets 8 MB.
- **File ▾ › Level Links…** shows how Spyro gets into every level and back out, on one page: each homeworld's doorways with a **leads to** list (the game's levels, your new level, and levels you built, by their own names), and for every level where **Return Home** (its vortex, or Exit Level in the pause menu) brings him back out, with whether the level has its Return Home vortex. Send a doorway to a level and Return Home from that level comes back out of that doorway; the level it led to before comes back out of the doorway that still leads there. Plain sentences say what is wrong (a level no doorway leads to, two doorways to one level, a level with no vortex, a link Create BIN can't make, with a button to put it right), and your world's entrance, doorways and way home are summed up. Changes are saved at once, with Undo Last Change; **Open Level Links…** in Game Settings › Portals & Return Home opens it too.
- After **Random Level** makes a level, its window says how Spyro gets there and back; for the project's new level, which no doorway leads to yet, **Choose Its Doorway…** opens Level Links on it.
- Create BIN places the way into your new world by itself when there is none yet (on open ground near where Spyro arrives in the homeworld; move it with Terrain › World Entrance). It used to stop there.
- Fix It For Me leaves out far less. When a level's objects stop Create BIN, it now finds which **kinds of object you added** are the cause (every copy of one creature, say) and leaves out only those; your other objects and your changes to the level's own objects stay in the game. It tries the added kinds in halves, which takes a few more runs of Create BIN.
- When Fix It For Me has left anything out, a window opens that says what and why, with Put Back beside each thing. It is no longer only a line at the bottom of the window.
- After Create BIN the status says how much room the levels you added objects to have left, the fullest first, and warns when one is nearly full.

Fixed

- In random levels the ground could show **hairline gaps with the sky behind them** just behind Spyro as he ran (dashed lines across the floor, worst on open grass). The game cuts the tiles nearest the camera into sixteen pieces and the next ones into four, and where those meet the extra corners do not land exactly on the edge; the game's own levels hide it with bigger floor tiles. A random level's sky is now painted the ground's colour below the horizon, so nothing shows through (measured in the emulator: the bright gaps are gone). Sky islands keep their own sky.
- Random objects asked for **teal or rainbow gems** with some level types' objects (Dark Passage's) said Create BIN would refuse the arrangement and to press Randomize again, which never helped. It now makes the arrangement again at once without them, their part of the treasure in ordinary gems, and says why.
- **A locked chest's key from Random objects lay half sunk in the floor** and was easy to miss. Keys now float as high as the game's own (19 units), wherever they are added.
- The Random Level window could be taller than the screen, with Make Level under the Dock and no way to scroll to it. Make Level, Cancel and where to put the level now stay at the window's foot while the choices above scroll, the window can be made smaller, and every editor window that opens over another is kept inside the screen.
- Moving a point of one of the game's own egg thieves' runs made Create BIN stop ("egg-thief path export blocked … clearance"), even for a small drag: the check compared heights the game never uses, and edits to the game's own runs were refused anyway. Such runs are now judged the way the game runs them.

- A spring chest added with Add Object (a plain one too) made Create BIN stop in Stone Hill with "Object build blocked by the native Moby safety check" when the project had no level you built: it was wrongly given the safety profile of an older way of importing chests. Chests from Add Object are checked by their own fit check now, and a project saved with such a chest is made whole as it is.
- A doorway with a shape (Terrain › Doorways, or a generated homeworld) sometimes did not load its level: Spyro was lifted into it and then flew on through and fell. The game flies Spyro in along its original doorway's path at the original's height, and Stone Hill's from Artisans runs high, along the top of a smaller shape's opening. Spyro now flies in through the lower part of every shaped opening (checked for every shape at every size on every homeworld's doorways), and Create BIN builds the way into your new world the same way.
- A homeworld with the way into your new world and an added object that brings its own sounds or sparks (a Crash frog or crate, a creature from another level) made Create BIN stop with "New World doorway failed its readback: particles and sounds changed". Fix It For Me then got the game made by leaving out every object change of that level. The check was wrong, not the level: such levels are made whole now.
- The export check said "Ready to export" when all it had checked was your object changes. It now says what it checked, and that Create BIN checks memory, doorways and your world while it builds.
